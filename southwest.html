Southwest vs. American Airlines: Competitive Performance

A year of U.S. on-time data, two very different airlines, and one question: where do delays really come from?

Overview

The project benchmarks Southwest Airlines (WN) against American Airlines (AA) using the 2024 on-time performance data published by the Bureau of Transportation Statistics. The goal was to understand how each carrier's delays behave and how their route networks explain the difference, then present it as interactive visuals a stakeholder can read without a data background.

How it was built

  1. 1

    Combine the monthly files

    The 12 monthly BTS files for 2024 were too large to work with whole. A Python (pandas) notebook reads each file, keeps only the 23 columns needed, and filters to Southwest and American, then writes one combined dataset.

  2. 2

    Keep the columns that explain delay

    Flight date, carrier, origin and destination (airport, city, state), departure and arrival times, taxi-out and taxi-in, cancelled and diverted flags, and delay minutes split by cause: carrier, weather, national air system, security, and late aircraft.

  3. 3

    Prepare the data in SQL

    SQL joins, subqueries, and GROUP BY aggregations turned the raw records into a clean base for benchmarking the two carriers.

  4. 4

    Build the views

    Tableau worksheets for arrival delay, departure delay (total and percentage), and security delay (total and percentage), plus a network comparison and a summary table. Two animated views plot departure delay against arrival delay and taxi-in time against departure delay, each wrapped in a story dashboard.

  5. 5

    Add Power BI views

    Interactive Power BI and Tableau views surface route efficiency, on-time rates, and cost metrics to support strategic forecasting.

  6. 6

    Write the takeaway on every view

    Each visual carries a short annotation so the point is clear without a presenter.

What the data showed

Every finding below is taken from the annotations on the final dashboards.

  • American shows about three times the arrival delay of Southwest, which points to stronger performance issues.
  • American also has higher departure delays, which the dashboard ties to congestion and extended disruptions.
  • American runs a wider, more distributed hub-and-spoke network. Southwest runs a dense point-to-point network built around fewer central hubs.
  • Security delay is negligible for both carriers, so airport security is not a major factor.
  • Southwest has more carrier delay events overall, but a much lower average departure delay (11.7 minutes). Its delays are shorter and better controlled.
  • American's hub-and-spoke structure creates longer disruptions when delays happen, so its total departure delay is much higher.

Takeaway

  • Southwest has more delay events, but they are shorter. American has fewer, but they last longer and ripple through the network. The difference comes down to how each airline's network is built.
